### A note on who qualifies as an author

Authorship should require a **meaningful intellectual or research contribution** — for example
shaping the research question or design, building or validating the measurement and modelling
work, interpreting results, or drafting and critically revising the manuscript — together with
accountability for the work and agreement to the final version.

**Narrower assistance is better recognised in acknowledgments than in the author list.** Examples
that usually belong in acknowledgments rather than authorship: providing data access or funding
alone, routine technical or editorial help, commenting on a draft without shaping it, or general
supervision unaccompanied by substantive contribution. This is a judgement call for the project
owner; the point is that the two categories exist and should be used deliberately rather than by
default.

If a tool or automated assistant contributed to drafting or analysis, disclose that in
§6 (Acknowledgments) or per the venue's policy. Tools are not authors.
