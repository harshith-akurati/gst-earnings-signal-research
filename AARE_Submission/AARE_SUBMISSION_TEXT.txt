TITLE
Search Attention Without Strategy: Association, Prediction, and Tradability Around Earnings Announcements

ABSTRACT
Public information can be associated with market activity without supplying incremental forecasts or tradable returns. This study examines weekly Google search attention around 285 usable United States earnings announcements from 28 companies between March 2021 and December 2025. High attention is fixed before outcomes as a weekly Google Trends spike at least 5 rolling standard deviations above the prior 12 week mean, with the event week excluded from its own baseline.

In a matched comparison of 73 high attention earnings events against 73 individually matched control events, high attention events had absolute event day returns 2.08 percentage points larger, p = .004. This retrospective association does not establish incremental predictive value. A separate ticker clustered regression over the full 285 event population produced a coefficient of 1.51 percentage points, p = .066, with a confidence interval spanning zero. These two analyses use different samples and different estimands, so they are not competing estimates of one quantity.

Every incremental test was null. Against a price and calendar baseline, the marginal contribution of attention was 0.01 percentage points, p = .98. Out of sample magnitude error was 3.63 percentage points with attention and 3.63 without, paired p = .98. A directional model was worse calibrated than an intercept only base rate. A standalone attention filtered event strategy returned 0.85 percent per event, but comparable post earnings drift occurred without the attention filter, so that return is not attributable to attention.

Association, prediction, and tradability must be evaluated as separate empirical claims.
