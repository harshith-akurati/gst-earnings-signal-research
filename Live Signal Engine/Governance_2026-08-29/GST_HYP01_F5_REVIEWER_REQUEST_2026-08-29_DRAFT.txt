Hello,

I am writing to ask whether you would be willing to act as the independent
reviewer for one bounded methodological question in an observational finance

WHAT THE STUDY DOES

It tests whether abnormal pre-earnings Google-search attention is associated
with a larger absolute overnight price move at an earnings announcement. It is
an association study across 24 large US issuers, targeting 60 events. It makes
no causal or trading claim. The statistical analysis is already fixed and
internally authorized; no data has been collected and no result exists.

THE QUESTION I NEED REVIEWED

Our attention measure is built from a Google Trends series. The production code
currently uses the BARE TICKER SYMBOL as the search query for every issuer --
"AAPL", "DELL", "GME" and so on. There is no mapping layer; the ticker IS the
query.

An internal audit flagged this as unresolved and could not close it. It was an
offline lexical screen with zero live Trends calls, and it explicitly forbids
its own use to close the item. The concerns it raised:

  * Does a bare ticker measure interest in the ISSUER, or investor-framed
    interest only -- a much narrower thing than public attention?
  * Lexical collisions. "DELL" is an ordinary word and a consumer brand.
    "GME", "STZ" and "BKR" were flagged specifically.
  * Coverage and sparsity: some queries may return series too thin to
    standardize.
  * Punctuation handling, e.g. BRK-B versus BRK.B.
  * Whether a Google topic/entity behaves differently from free text.
  * Google Trends' own revision behaviour, which is undocumented here.

The audit marks empirical comparison MANDATORY for 11 tickers -- BKR, BRK-B,
DELL, DVN, GME, GOOGL, GPN, NKE, STZ, SYK, UAL -- and recommended for the
other 13.

WHY IT MATTERS THAT SOMEONE OUTSIDE THE PROJECT DOES THIS

A query change changes the signal. Whoever picks the query is defining the
predictor. If the analysis side picks it, the study is choosing its own
measurement, and the preregistration is worth much less.

I should be direct about one constraint that shapes the whole task: the
selection must be OUTCOME-BLIND. You would compare candidate queries on search
data only, over a fixed historical window declared in advance. You may not
compare them against prices, returns or earnings responses, and a query may
never be preferred because it predicts a market move better. That would make
our predictor a function of the outcome.

WHAT THE WORK INVOLVES

  * Predeclare selection criteria before pulling anything.
  * For each ticker, compare the same six candidate query families over one
    fixed development-only interval.
  * Assess six dimensions: issuer specificity, lexical collision, coverage,
    punctuation behaviour, topic availability, and repeat-pull revision
    behaviour (the same query pulled on two separate days).
  * Recommend one systematic rule for all 24, or justify each exception in
    writing.
  * Retain every candidate and result, including rejected ones.
  * Produce one dated artifact stating whether the construct-validity item is
    closed.

Full protocol, the 24-row query inventory with our offline screen, and the
governing documents are in the attached packet. Scope is bounded: it is a query
mapping decision, not an audit of the study.

WHAT I AM AND AM NOT ASKING

I am asking you to decide what the queries should be, and to say so in writing.
I am not asking you to endorse the study, its hypothesis or its analysis, and
your review would not imply that.

If the honest answer is that the bare ticker is inadequate and every query must
change, that is a completely acceptable outcome -- it just has to be recorded
before we collect anything.

WHAT I CAN TELL YOU ABOUT INDEPENDENCE

The earlier screen was produced by an AI assistant at my direction and states
plainly that it is not a search-methodology authority. Neither that assistant
nor I can serve as the qualified reviewer here. That is precisely why I am
writing to someone outside the project.

TIMING
